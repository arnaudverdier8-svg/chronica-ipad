## 0. North star (read this if nothing else)

**A real, physical, slightly worn embroidered frieze, filmed on a motion-control rostrum. The cloth wakes up through light and thread, not through cartoon animation.** Everything that moves does so in one of these ways:
(a) light moves across the cloth,
(b) thread is stitched on or pulled out,
(c) the cloth itself sways, folds, burns or frays,
(d) stitched figures move like rigid cut-outs, on twos or threes.
The camera never pretends the cloth is a 3D world. It tracks along it, pushes into it and pulls back from it.

Ten commandments:
1. **The linen always shows.** Bare ground covers 35 to 60 % of any frame wider than one figure. Faces are bare linen with an outline (Bayeux convention).
2. **Two registers of embroidery.** *Narrative* work is matte wool on linen with low relief (laid and couched fills, stem-stitch outlines, the dye palette). *Heraldic* work is crests, banners, crowns and the title. It is raised satin plus gold, with higher relief, more sheen and game-saturated colour. This split is how the muted Bayeux palette and the saturated game art coexist.
3. **Every fill is outlined** in blue-black (or red-brown) stem stitch. The outline never matches the fill exactly: gaps of 0 to 1 mm show linen.
4. **Limited palette per scene.** At most 6 dye colours plus linen plus ink in any shot. Repeated elements alternate colours (Bayeux principle): ship planks, horse legs, roof tiles, a row of knights.
5. **Raking light is the default.** Key elevation is 15 to 30 deg. Never leave a flat frontal key on screen for more than 2 s, except for a deliberate "museum record" beat.
6. **No perfect regularity anywhere.** Use the jitter ranges in section 4.6.
7. **Wool is matte with a soft sheen. Only metal thread glints.** No plastic specular highlights.
8. **Read left to right.** Time moves rightward (camera trucks right, content slides left). Moving right-to-left is reserved for regression: the war and ruin act.
9. **Fibres, not pixels.** Every wool edge gets a 0.2 to 0.4 mm fuzz halo. Stray fibres catch grazing light.
10. **Age with restraint.** Stains, foxing, creases, a few nail holes and one or two mends per panel, concentrated at the edges. Never so much that it reads as filth.

### 1.2 Stitches actually used
- **Laid and couched work ("Bayeux stitch")** fills nearly every coloured area. Long strands are *laid* side by side across the whole shape and surface only. Then *couching bars* of the same yarn are laid at right angles over them at intervals. Each bar is pinned by small *tie-down stitches*. Almost no yarn sits on the back, which made it economical for a 68 m object. Laid work usually has 3 layers (laid, crossing bars, tie-downs); plain couching has 2.
- **Stem stitch / outline stitch** is used for all contours, details, hair, mail-coat hatching and **all lettering**. It is a rope of short overlapping slanted stitches.
- **Split stitch** and other stitches are rare, mostly in details and repairs. Do not use chain stitch, satin stitch or French knots in the *narrative* register.
- No metal thread exists in Bayeux. Gold is a *Chronica* addition, justified by contemporary English **opus anglicanum**, which used underside-couched silver-gilt. Use it only in the heraldic register.

### 1.3 Depiction conventions to borrow (and how)

| element | Bayeux convention | CHRONICA use |
|---|---|---|
| Figures | Profile or three-quarter, gesturing, pointing toward the next scene. Hierarchic scale (important people larger). Bodies are laid fill with garments in contrasting colours | Lords at the table, the king. Keep gesture legible in silhouette |
| Faces / flesh | **Bare linen** with stem-stitch outline. Eyes are a dot or short stroke. Hair and beards are filled | Narrative register: linen faces. The AI panels have filled, modelled faces; leave them, but do not add new modelled faces |
| Horses | Outlined, filled in non-naturalistic colours (blue, green, mustard, terracotta). **Far legs in a different colour from near legs** to show depth | Armies and the war act. Alternate colours along a cavalry line |
| Ships | Long crescent hulls. Clinker planks in **alternating colours**. Dragon prows, striped sails, shields along the gunwale | Not in the narration. Possible border vignette |
| Water | Paired wavy lines in olive green, turning red-brown or yellow where they cross hulls | Rivers on the map |
| Buildings | Cut-away/section views, arches, towers with domes. Roof tiles as scale patterns in alternating colours | Cities that fall (p6 panel). Castle silhouettes |
| Trees | Stylised interlace trees with banded trunks (each zone a different colour). **Scene dividers** | Our wipe device (section 7.4) and the "forest swallows the roads" beat |

## 3. CHRONICA cloth spec (scale, derived from the existing panels)

## 4. Physical appearance (what the camera must see)

### 4.1 Linen tabby
Plain over-under weave with **slubs** (thick, irregular stretches), slightly wandering threads, and small gaps between threads (the backing or shadow shows through). Warp is straighter than weft. Colour varies per thread by about +/-2.5 % L and over areas of about 12 mm by +/-2.5 %. Linen has a faint, tight sheen along the threads (Kajiya-Kay exponent about 24, intensity 0.05). Under raking light the weave reads as a fine basket texture. Under frontal light it nearly disappears (`light_compare.png`).

### 4.2 Laid and couched fill under raking light
- Long parallel laid strands, each a rounded ridge (height about 0.5x width) with **diagonal ply ridges**: S-plied 2-ply shows ridges at about 30 deg to the yarn axis, spaced about 0.7 mm.
- The strands are **pinched** where couching bars cross: the laid height dips by about 30 % within plus or minus 1 mm of a bar.
- Couching bars run perpendicular, each a single strand sitting on top. Between tie-downs they are straight; at each tie-down they kink and pinch by about 40 %.
- Tie-downs are tiny stitches across the bar, slightly proud.
- Strand ends tuck into the linen at the shape edge (rounded falloff over about 0.5 mm). The fill edge wanders by plus or minus 0.3 mm against the outline.
- A raking light gives every bar a hard 1 to 2 mm cast shadow on the strands below. This bar grid is *the* visual signature of the Bayeux. Show it in at least one close shot.

### 4.3 Stem-stitch outline
A **rope**: a row of short diagonal segments, each overlapping the next by half. Each stitch dips into the cloth at both ends, so the rope has a periodic bumpiness. It casts a continuous shadow on the side away from the light. Lettering is made the same way, so stroke ends are blunt rather than serifed, and curves are faceted into about 3.5 mm chords.

### 4.4 Fibre fuzz and halo
Wool fibres are about 20 to 35 um thick, with flyaways 0.5 to 3 mm long. At F1 and wider they read as a **soft halo** extending 0.2 to 0.4 mm beyond every wool edge (about 15 to 35 % opacity, in the local wool colour). At F2 and F3, individual curling fibres become visible, lit brighter at grazing angles (back-lit rim). Fuzz is densest at edges and on couching bars (abrasion). Without fuzz, wool reads as clay or plastic.

### 4.5 Sheen: wool vs. metal
- **Wool:** broad, weak, anisotropic sheen elongated across the fibre direction. Primary lobe 4 to 7 % with exponent about 8, plus a coloured secondary lobe (light passing through the fibre) 5 to 6 % with exponent about 3, tinted by the albedo, plus a grazing sheen about 12 %. Never a white hotspot.
- **Silk or floss (heraldic satin):** a narrower, stronger anisotropic lobe (exponent 30 to 60, 10 to 20 %). Direction changes per satin block, so blocks flash differently as the light moves. This is the game-crest look.
- **Couched metal (silver-gilt) thread:** a thin, very anisotropic metallic highlight (exponent 150 to 400, intensity 0.8 to 1.4, tinted by `#E9BE6A`), with a low diffuse term (about 25 %). Couched in pairs, with tie-downs in coloured silk every 2 to 3 mm that interrupt the glint. Tarnished stretches drop to `#7A5A2A`. As the light moves, the glint **travels along the thread**. Use this for the crown and the title sweep.

| Laid strand position / width / direction | +/-8 % pitch, +/-12 % width, +/-2.5 deg (smooth over about 10 mm) |

### 4.7 Ageing (scale the 2020 survey, then restrain)
The survey found 24,204 stains, 9,646 losses and 16,445 creases over about 34 m2: roughly **7 stains, 3 losses and 5 creases per 10x10 cm**, mostly millimetre-sized and concentrated at the start and the edges. For the cinematic, use **20 to 40 % of that density**:
- **Foxing:** 0.5 to 3 mm brown spots, multiply 15 to 35 %, clustered.
- **Tidelines:** water-stain rings 2 to 10 cm, with a darker 1 to 2 mm edge.
- **Wax drips:** glossy, raised 0.2 mm. A candle motif for the oath and death scenes.
- **Nail holes:** along the top border every 20 to 40 cm, each with a small brown rust halo.
- **Losses:** missing wool with linen showing, or a hole through to the backing `#C7B48C`.
- **Mends:** one or two per panel. Visible darning in a slightly mismatched, *faded* thread (`#E2DBC6`, blue-turned-green `#8FA08C`).
- **Loose threads:** 1 to 3 per panel, 5 to 20 mm long, lifting off the surface. These become animation hooks.
- **Creases:** soft horizontal folds as low-frequency height (0.3 to 1 mm over 5 to 20 mm).
- **Fading:** greatest on blues.

### 4.8 Museum photography vs. our lighting
The real object is shown at textile-conservation light levels (about 50 lux, warm, diffuse, behind glass). Official photography is flat, so its relief is invisible. Conservators use **raking light (5 to 15 deg)** to read topography. We borrow the conservator's raking light as our expressive tool. A deliberate "museum record" beat (flat light, still, slight glass reflection) is allowed once, for example as a cold open.

## 5. Rendering recommendations (this machine: 4 shared CPUs, no GPU, Blender 4.0.2 Eevee-legacy under xvfb)

### 5.1 Recommended pipeline: 2.5D maps plus a numpy relight (primary)
For every stitched layer (panels, new procedural elements, tituli, borders), produce aligned maps at **2x the final on-screen pixel density for F0/F1**:
`albedo (linear)`, `height_mm`, `tangent (2-ch, thread direction)`, `material id (linen / wool / silk / metal)`, `wool coverage` (for fuzz/halo), and optionally `stitch_id` (for stitch-on animation).
Relight per frame only when the light moves. When only the camera moves, relight once, cache the shaded plate, and transform it.

Shading model as implemented in `tools/stitch_ref.py`. Units are mm; the screen is x right, y down, z toward the camera.
1. Normal: `N = normalize(-dh/dx, -dh/dy, 1)` after a 0.6 px Gaussian on the height. For F0 framings, scale the gradient by the LOD factor from section 3.
2. Diffuse: wrap Lambert, `(N.L + 0.25)/1.25`.
3. Shadow: march the height field toward the light, 60 steps x 1.25 px (covers 3.7 mm at 20 px/mm). Occluded when `h(p + s*l2d) > h(p) + s*tan(elev)`. Soft visibility `= clamp(1 - excess/0.12 mm)`, then a 1.2 px blur. This alone gives the couching-bar shadow grid.
4. AO: `clamp(1 - 1.6*max(0, blur(h, 0.8 mm) - h), 0.4, 1)`.
5. Specular (Kajiya-Kay): orthogonalise T to N; `sinTH = sqrt(1-(T.H)^2)`. Per material (exponent / intensity):
   - linen: 24 / 0.05
   - wool: 8 / 0.045, plus a coloured lobe 3 / 0.06 x albedo, plus grazing sheen `0.12*(1-Nz)^2`
   - silk satin: 40 / 0.15
   - metal: 180 / 1.4 tinted by the gold F0, with diffuse x0.25
   Fade all of it in with `smoothstep(-0.1, 0.25, N.L)`.
6. Light colours: key `(1.0, 0.92, 0.80) x 2.3`, fill `(0.80, 0.86, 1.0) x 0.30`, multiplied by the act tints in 2.4.
7. Fuzz: halo is the wool colour blurred over wool only (sigma 0.3 mm), composited outside wool at 35 % of `(blurred_coverage - coverage)`. Fibres: about 0.7 per mm2 of patch, edge-weighted x8. Each is a 4 to 22 segment random walk (1.5 px steps, curvature sigma 0.25 rad), 1 px anti-aliased, alpha 0.25 to 0.5, coloured by the local wool colour x1.15. Re-seed fibres per shot, not per frame.
8. Tone: ACES fit (Narkowicz) at exposure 0.85, then sRGB. Then the grade from 2.4.

Cost: `stitch_ref.py` builds the maps from scratch **and** shades two 960x720 images in about 5 s on one core. A 2560x1440 relight from cached maps should be about 3 to 6 s/frame single-threaded (estimate). Relight only during light-sweep shots, and parallelise by frame.

### 5.2 Making the AI panels hold up (p1, p3, p6)
The panels already contain baked stitch texture and chiaroscuro. Their fills are directional long strands following form (needle-painting, closer to split or long-and-short stitch), **not** laid and couched. So:
1. Estimate the thread direction field with a structure tensor on luminance (sigma about 2 to 3 px panel-native). The thread direction is the eigenvector with the smaller eigenvalue.
2. Synthesise a strand height field by **line integral convolution** (LIC) of white noise along that field (kernel 12 to 20 px), high-passed. Add a pitch-matched sinusoid across strands where coherence is above 0.5. Optionally take a panel-luminance high-pass at 10 to 20 % weight; Redon et al. used a related RGB-to-normal approach on the Bayeux.
3. Relight **as a ratio**, so baked shading is not doubled: `out = panel x (shade(N_synth, L) / shade(N_flat, L))`, clamped to 0.6 to 1.5. Add the anisotropic specular from the synthesised tangents.
4. Add only the new fine-scale layers (fuzz halo, raking micro-shadows, ageing) on top. Do not push past **1.4x** panel-native (7 px/mm) without this re-synthesis. Past **2.5x**, cut to a procedural or 3D insert.
5. New Bayeux-idiom elements (laid and couched with bars) go where they do not sit next to panel fills at the same scale: borders, banners, tituli, the map, the blank-page beat.

### 5.3 Blender 4.0.2 (Eevee legacy) notes, for when you need real parallax or geometry
- Eevee legacy **ignores anisotropy** and **material displacement** (bump only). For silhouettes, use real geometry: a Subdivision plus Displace modifier from `height_mm`. Use **2 to 4 vertices per yarn width** only where the silhouette is seen (macro patch: a 50x50 mm patch at 0.25 mm spacing is 200x200 = 40k vertices). Elsewhere use normal maps.
- Work at real scale, 1 BU = 1 m (0.8 mm yarn = 0.0008), so camera DOF behaves physically. Displacement amplitude is 0.4 to 0.6x yarn width for wool fills, 0.15x for linen, and 2 to 3x for padded heraldic satin.
- Principled BSDF v2 starting points:
  - **wool:** base = dye colour; roughness 0.80 to 0.90; IOR 1.55, IOR-level about 0.35; sheen weight 0.5 to 0.8, sheen roughness 0.35 to 0.5, sheen tint about 0.5x albedo + 0.5 white (the 4.0 sheen is the Zeltner/Burley/Chiang microfibre model); coat 0; subsurface 0 in Eevee (cost), or weight 0.05 to 0.1 with a 0.2 mm radius in Cycles; anisotropic 0.6 with the tangent along the yarn (Cycles only).
  - **linen:** roughness 0.75, sheen 0.2, IOR-level 0.3.
  - **silk satin:** roughness 0.35 to 0.45, anisotropic 0.8 (Cycles), sheen 0.3.
  - **metal gold:** metallic 1, base `#E9BE6A`, roughness 0.25 to 0.35, anisotropic 0.8 along the thread (Cycles); in Eevee, fake the elongated glint with a stretched bump.
- Fuzz in 3D: hair particles (strands) on the wool, 30 to 60 per cm2, length 0.5 to 2.5 mm, root radius 15 um, rendered as strands. Expensive under llvmpipe, so use it only in F3 macro shots and keep 2D fuzz elsewhere. Cycles needs OIDN, which is missing, so it is noisy; prefer Eevee plus the 2D fuzz composite.
- Test render budget for other agents: keep stills at 1280x720, 8 to 16 TAA samples. v1 measured about 15 s/frame at 1280x720 with 10 samples.

### 5.4 Why CG embroidery looks fake, and the fix
| symptom | cause | fix (numbers) |
|---|---|---|
| "Corduroy" or machine look | perfectly regular strand pitch, straight parallel bars | jitter from 4.6. Bars slant +/-4 deg and kink at tie-downs |
| Plastic or clay | isotropic Blinn or GGX highlight with a white tint; no fibre detail | Kajiya-Kay along the tangent, intensity of 0.06 or less for wool, coloured secondary lobe, fibre micro-relief (about 0.025 mm streak noise along the strand), ply-twist ridges |
| Flat "print" look | uniform colour per region | per-strand L +/-4.5 % C +/-8 %, along-strand noise, dye-lot patches, woad wear |
| Sticker or vector edges | fill exactly equals the outline, edges anti-aliased and perfect | fills stop 0 to 0.5 mm short or overlap. Strand ends tuck in over 0.5 mm. Fuzz halo 0.2 to 0.4 mm |
| No fibre life | no flyaways | 0.5 to 1 flyaways per mm2 at edges, rim-lit at grazing |
| Over-inflated relief | height 1x yarn width or more; huge shadows | laid 0.5x width; outline 0.7 to 1.2 mm total; raking at 15 to 30 deg, not 5 deg (except deliberate reveals) |
| Shimmer during moves | sub-2 px weave or strand frequency | LOD fade (section 3), render at 2x, motion blur |
| Too clean | no ageing; pure white linen | linen `#D4BE98` base, ageing per 4.7, white clip `#F3E8D0` |
| "3D world" look | camera rotates around the cloth; deep parallax | keep the cloth plane within 25 deg of facing the camera. Lift-off at most 5 to 15 mm. Track, don't orbit |
| Heraldry and narrative clash | same material everywhere | two registers (section 0, item 2) |
| Lettering looks typeset | font glyphs filled solid | stem-stitch along glyph skeletons (3.5 mm chords), wobbling baseline +/-0.8 mm, letter height +/-6 % |

## 6. Lighting rules
1. **Key:** raking from **upper-left** (azimuth about 135 deg, light coming from 10 to 11 o'clock), elevation **15 to 30 deg** (default 22). Shadow length = height / tan(elevation): about 1.4 mm for 0.5 mm wool at 20 deg.
2. **Fill:** cool, soft, at a 1:2.5 to 1:6 ratio to the key, depending on the act (2.4). Never remove all fill: the linen must never go muddy.
3. **Rim / back-graze:** a 5 to 10 deg grazing light from the upper right at 20 to 30 % of the key, to light the fuzz halo. Use it on hero shots.
4. **Light sweeps** are the primary "awakening" device. Moving the key azimuth 60 to 120 deg over 2 to 4 s makes stitches catch light in sequence and the metal glint travel. Sweep **left to right** for progress and right to left for decline.
5. **Practicals** (diegetic light on the cloth):
   - Candles, 1900 K: two noise bands, 1 to 3 Hz sway plus 8 to 12 Hz flicker, 6 to 12 % amplitude, soft pools about 15 to 25 cm across.
   - Fire, 2200 K: 2 to 6 Hz, 15 to 25 %, from below.
   - Dawn, 4300 K: an even 3 to 4 s ramp.
6. **Flat frontal light (elevation above 50 deg)** is for the "museum record" beat only (`light_compare.png`, right).
7. **Vignette:** physical, from light falloff on the cloth, not a darkened overlay. In Act III the edges go darker *and* the cloth itself frays and unravels there.
8. Avoid pure-black shadows (floor `#07070A`) and pure-white highlights (ceiling `#F3E8D0`). Metal glints may reach `#FFF3D6` for 1 to 3 frames.

### 7.2 Camera grammar for a frieze
- **Truck, don't pan.** Move the camera parallel to the cloth. This keeps the plane undistorted and every stitch in focus.
- **Speed limits at 30 fps.** Over stitch texture, move at most **12 to 15 px/frame** (about 360 to 450 px/s at master, so an element takes 5.5 to 7 s to cross the frame). Faster moves need motion blur with a 180 deg equivalent (blur = 0.5 x displacement per frame). Ramp in and out over at least 0.8 s with cubic ease. A held frame needs at least 1 to 2 s of slow drift (2 to 4 px/frame), because a dead-still textile reads as a photo.
- **Scale ladder (reveals).** F3 macro (one couching bar or a tie-down) to F2 (figure) to F1 (scene) to F0 (frieze strip with borders). Pull-backs of 4x to 10x over 3 to 6 s use exponential zoom (constant perceived speed): `scale(t) = s0*(s1/s0)^ease(t)`. The reverse (push-in) is for intimacy: the empty chair, the crown, "you".
- **DOF as the scale cue** (full-frame 36 mm sensor, circle of confusion 0.03 mm). Depth of field ≈ `2*N*c*(m+1)/m^2`:
  - 50 mm field (m 0.72): **1.6 mm at f/8**, 3.2 mm at f/16
  - 200 mm field: about 17 mm at f/8
  - 550 mm field: about 12 cm
  So macro shots *must* show focus falloff. Tilt the cloth 15 to 25 deg relative to the lens so a sharp band of about 5 mm crosses the frame. Wide shots are sharp everywhere.
- **Angle.** The cloth plane stays within 25 deg of facing the camera, so 2.5D plates hold. Use a 5 to 15 deg tilt to show relief and the hanging rod. Roll 0 deg, except a 2 to 4 deg dutch angle in Act III.
- **Parallax inside the cloth.** Lift only what is "awakened": figures or objects rise 5 to 15 mm, a finger's breadth, and cast soft shadows on the linen. Everything else stays at stitch relief.
- **Physicality.** The tapestry may hang and sway: at most 1 to 2 % of height, period 3 to 6 s. Billow or fold only at transitions.

### 7.3 Motion vocabulary ("awakening")
- **Stitch-on.** Thread appears along the needle path at 20 to 60 mm/s physical. Each stitch pops in over 2 to 3 frames with height 0 to 110 % to 100 %, led by a glint (steel needle or metal thread). Fills: laid strands first, then couching bars, then tie-downs, then the outline (the real order).
- **Unpick / unravel.** The reverse. The loose end lifts and curls in 3D (at most 20 mm). Couching bars release first, then laid strands slacken (height ripple), then pull out.
- **Thread boil.** On animated elements only, re-seed the jitter every 2 frames (15 fps feel) or 3 (10 fps), at the 4.6 amplitudes. The static cloth never boils.
- **Figures.** Rigid cut-out parts rotating about joints, 2 to 4 poses, holds of 6 to 12 frames, poses changing on twos. Squash and stretch at most 5 %. Gestures point the eye rightward to the next scene.
- **Elements.**
  - Flames: laid strands shifting phase (2 to 4 Hz), with outer strands lagging.
  - Smoke: stem-stitch spirals (as in p6) rotating 10 to 20 deg/s and drifting up.
  - Water: paired wavy lines with phase travelling left to right.
  - Forest growth: green stem-stitch tendrils and couched leaves stitching over the roads (Act III, 35 to 37 s).
- **Colour revival** (Act IV): aged to fresh dye (2.4), staggered at 0.2 to 0.4 s per region, following the light sweep.

### 7.4 Transitions (diegetic, textile)
| device | when |
|---|---|
| **Tree-divider wipe**: truck past a stylised interlace tree that fills the frame for 6 to 12 frames | scene changes inside an act (Bayeux's own scene divider) |
| **Border escape**: follow a border beast or vine, then drop into the next scene | linking beats (e.g. lions from `vignette_*`) |
| **Unravel / restitch**: threads pull out of scene A and re-stitch as scene B | death to war, ruin to forest |
| **Burn-through**: scorch spreading from fire stitches, linen browning (`soot`) and opening a hole onto the next panel | 32.7 to 34 s "Cities fell to ruin" |
| **Fray-out**: frame edges unravel, weft threads pull loose, darkness behind | 42 to 45 s "the world grew dark at its edges" |
| **Fold / turn**: the cloth folds over like a page | Act IV "blank page", to a bare linen field with underdrawing only |
| **Sheen sweep to title**: gold glint travels along the title letters | 61.6 s onward to the end |
Hard cuts are fine on strong narration words (for example "died" @15.64, "wars" @28.34, "Now" @46.08) if both frames share light direction and scale tier.

### 7.6 Lettering: stitched Latin tituli (optional, high value)

Letters are about one tenth of the register height (about 3 cm; estimate). Build them from `fonts/cinzel-latin-700-normal.woff2`: skeletonise each glyph, stem-stitch along the skeleton, and apply the irregularities in 5.4. Do not show a raw font.

## 8. Do / Don't

**Do**
- Keep linen visible: faces in bare linen, gaps between fill and outline.
- Outline every fill. Alternate colours on repeated elements.
- Keep the narrative register matte (wool) and the heraldic register raised, sheened and saturated.
- Use a raking key at 15 to 30 deg and move the light to make things come alive.
- Truck laterally at 12 to 15 px/frame or less. Use exponential zoom for scale reveals. Use real DOF in macro shots.
- Animate through thread (stitch-on, unpick, boil on twos) and through rigid cut-out poses.
- Apply 20 to 40 % of real ageing density, concentrated at edges. Include one or two mends and loose threads as animation hooks.
- Use the per-act colour script, and aged-to-fresh dye revival in Act IV.
- Prefilter weave and strand frequencies below 2.5 px on screen.
- Keep metal gold rare (under 3 % of pixels), with a glint that travels along the thread.

**Don't**
- Use pure white or pure black, or flood the frame with saturated heraldic colour.
- Use plastic or white isotropic specular on wool.
- Use perfect spacing, uniform colour, or vector-perfect edges.
- Orbit the camera around the cloth, add deep parallax, or add perspective to new stitched elements.
- Use smooth, fluid character animation, particle fire or smoke, or lens flares.
- Upscale AI panels beyond 1.4x without stitch re-synthesis, or beyond 2.5x at all.
- Add new modelled or shaded faces, satin-stitch narrative fills, or chain stitch or French knots in the narrative register.
- Use raw typeset text on the cloth.
- Use purple outside the king, or metal thread outside crown, sword, title and cord.
- Add more than 6 dye colours plus linen plus ink per shot.
